

I ran into a problem with _Celestine_Prophecy_ wherein the author (Jeff 
Foxworthy?) develops an "as if" process of reasoning.  For example, on
page 42 ---

"Experiments have revealed that when you break apart small aspects of this
 energy, what we call elementary particles, and try to observe how they
 operate, the act of observation itself alters the results - *as if* these
 elementary particles are influenced by what the experimenter *expects*.
 *This is true* even if the particles must appear in places ..."


The premise (the experiments he refers to), are true.  Things can only
be observed by touching them.  To see an object, you must touch it with
light.  If you are attempting to observe the smallest thing you know,
then the smallest thing you can touch it with is another thing of the
same size or larger.  This will certainly alter the results of the
experiment.

The "as if" conclusion has nothing to do with the premise.  A statement
such as "You were driving 120 MPH, (true, I measured it), *as if* you
wanted to kill yourself" is not an argument. Although a person who wants to
kill herself may do it by driving 120,  it does not mean that a person
who drives 120 wants to kill himself.  In fact,  this statement is a 
way of saying "look, my friend, we both know you don't want to die, so 
please stop driving at 120 mph."

If that is not bad enough, he attempts to give the particles conscious
behavior. "influenced by what the experimenter *expects*".  Giving
unconscious objects conscious behavior should be reserved for small
children with stuffed toys, and computer users with bad software.

To top it off he follows up his "as if" conclusion with a statement
claiming that "This is true ...".  What is true? the experiment or the
conclusion? He is ambiguous on this point, and gives the reader the
impression that *both* are true.  This is an unfair style of writing.

Other than that,  so far it is an interesting book.
